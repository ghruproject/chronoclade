# Interpretation

ChronoClade describes patterns in the sampled genomes. Public-health conclusions
still depend on the sampling process and epidemiological information that the
phylogeny does not contain.

## Evidence for local persistence

A local group sampled across the surveillance period is compatible with
continued circulation or repeated detection of a reservoir. Confidence grows
when the group remains intact after recombination correction, within-group
distances are smaller than between-group distances, and regional context does
not split the local isolates.

The same pattern can arise from repeated importation of a common, poorly sampled
source. A local-only tree cannot distinguish those explanations.

## Evidence for separate introductions

Several separated local groups, especially when interspersed with public or
regional genomes, are compatible with independent introductions. The number of
visible groups is not an estimate of the number of introductions. Unsampled
diversity can merge, split or reposition those groups when more genomes are
added.

## Role of the dated tree

A supported dated tree can place ancestral splits before, during or after the
local sampling period. It can test whether a proposed epidemiological sequence
is compatible with the evolutionary timescale. Wide node intervals may leave
several explanations open.

Temporal signal does not establish local circulation. Conversely, a failed
temporal test does not erase evidence in the corrected topology or distance
matrix; it prevents reliable conversion of genetic branch length into calendar
time.

## Questions to ask before acting

- Were isolates collected through stable surveillance or targeted outbreak
  sampling?
- Are date and location confounded with major genetic groups?
- Does each focal group have adequate public and regional context?
- Could repeated isolates from one patient dominate a group?
- Do node-date intervals distinguish the proposed timelines?
- Do patient movement, ward and exposure records agree with the genomic pattern?

## Language for reports

Use "consistent with" or "compatible with" when describing circulation and
introduction patterns. State the number of sampled genomic groups rather than
the number of transmission or introduction events. Report the date range,
number of genomes, context coverage and temporal-test result beside any dated
estimate.
